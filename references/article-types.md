# Article types

Use these patterns to draft an outline when the user doesn't give one, or to sanity-check one they did give. Headings stay plain. No conclusion section in any of them: the last section is an ordinary topic.

## Contents
- How-to / step-by-step
- Problem explainer
- Privacy and safety
- Tool roundup and honest comparison
- Utility and format (compress, convert, resize)
- Outline checks

## How-to / step-by-step

Sections: why the problem happens or matters, what goes wrong, the steps, how to get a good starting input, a common-mistakes or extra-tips section, a section on a practical edge case (privacy, file size, sharing).

Notes worth asking for: tool used, how long it took, what failed, the annoying part. Steps go in prose with First, Then, After that, Last.

## Problem explainer

Sections: what it looks like, the main causes (one section per cause, if they differ), what actually fixes each, what to do next time.

Keep causes concrete. People search these when they have a specific broken thing in front of them.

## Privacy and safety

Sections: what people upload and why it matters, what apps may do with files (general, unless verified facts are given), how browser-based processing differs (only if verified for the user's tool), when to be extra careful, how to check any tool's policy.

Highest risk of unverified claims. Follow `honesty-and-claims.md` strictly and stay generic where facts are missing.

## Tool roundup and honest comparison

Sections: what to look for, one section per tool, which fits which situation.

Write about other tools only from facts the user supplied. Comparison tables are produced separately (see `seo-extras.md`), not inside the article. Be fair: mention a real limitation of the user's own tool if they gave one, which also makes the article more believable.

## Utility and format (compress, convert, resize)

Sections: why the limit or requirement exists, why the obvious way fails, what actually changes the result (in order), a step-by-step method, how to check the result.

Give numbers as starting points ("start around 80") and tell the reader to test, since every file behaves differently.

## Outline checks

Before writing, confirm:
1. 5 to 7 H2 sections is enough for 900 words. More than 8 sections makes thin sections.
2. No item that duplicates another (steps and tips often do).
3. No wrap-up, FAQ, or table items in the body.
4. One primary keyword and one clear angle, different from other articles in `assets/site-profile.md`.
5. Every item can be written honestly with the notes available. If an item needs facts the user didn't give (competitor features, privacy behaviour), mark it generic or ask.
