# What editing actually buys you

Two studies in the last three years measured something most writing advice only asserts: that cleaner prose changes what experts decide, not just how the page feels.

## Editing moves the decision, not just the prose

Feld, Lines and Ross paid two professional editors to rework thirty economics papers, then had economists judge the originals against the edited versions. Writing experts scored the edited drafts 0.6 SD higher for overall writing quality. The economists, who never saw both versions of the same paper, were 8.4 percentage points more likely to accept an edited paper for a conference.

That is the number worth keeping. A copy-edit bought a measurable swing in an accept/reject decision made in five to eight minutes.

## The tool effect is real and it is uneven

Noy and Zhang gave 444 college-educated professionals a paid writing task and randomly handed ChatGPT to half of them. Time on the post-treatment task fell 37% and evaluator grades rose 0.45 SD. The gains went mostly to the weaker writers, which compresses the distribution rather than lifting it.

Put next to the editing study, that is a consistent story. Both interventions work on the same axis, and both help the draft that started worst.
