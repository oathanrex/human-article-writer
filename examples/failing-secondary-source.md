# Write like someone who read the research

Most advice about clear writing is a summary of a summary. This draft is the same, on purpose: it is what a piece looks like when it comes out of a notebook of book notes instead of out of the papers.

## The curse of knowledge

Experts forget what it is like not to know. They skip the step that seems obvious, define nothing, and hand the reader a page of conclusions with the reasoning missing.

## Long words do not read as smart

Swapping short words for thesaurus synonyms lowers how intelligent the writer looks, which is the opposite of the intent. Readers judge the writing by how easily it goes down.

## Editing and tooling both move the outcome

One experiment found that professional editing raised expert writing-quality scores by 0.6 SD, and that economists were 8.4 percentage points more likely to accept an edited paper for a conference. Another found that access to ChatGPT cut task time by 37% and raised output quality by 0.45 SD.
